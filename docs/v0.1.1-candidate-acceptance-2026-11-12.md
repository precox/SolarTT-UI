## Fresh source/security preflight, 12 November

All 129 Python tests passed. The project's source/history secret scans reported
zero findings. The RustSec audit completed successfully against advisory database
commit `af7b3ec33460b8e2de8d422e16d628518891a519`, with zero entries in its
vulnerability list. Its informational warnings remain visible, not suppressed:

- `rustls-pemfile` 1.0.4 and 2.2.0:
  [RUSTSEC-2025-0134](https://rustsec.org/advisories/RUSTSEC-2025-0134.html),
  unmaintained.
- `rustix` 1.1.5:
  [RUSTSEC-2026-0486](https://rustsec.org/advisories/RUSTSEC-2026-0486.html),
  unsound extended-attribute buffer handling; patched in 1.1.6.
- `tokio` 1.53.2:
  [RUSTSEC-2026-0386](https://rustsec.org/advisories/RUSTSEC-2026-0386.html),
  unsound `AsyncFd` descriptor ownership/lifetime contract; the current 1.53
  series is patched in 1.53.3.

These are dependency advisories, not proof of an exploitable VPN path. A direct
search of SolarTT's own crates found no calls to the affected xattr functions or
`AsyncFd` constructors; this does not establish absence throughout transitive
dependencies or every configuration. Their applicability and any dependency
update require review before stable publication. This metadata-only preparation
does not change the tested dependency graph or add an advisory ignore list.

## ACME staging, 11 November

`renew --dry-run --run-deploy-hooks` passed. The owner explicitly accepted the
actual Certbot Terms prompt and published each of the two real TXT challenges.
Both authoritative nameservers were checked for exact values before releasing

Dry-run deploy hooks use the active staging leaf. This does not establish actual
public-certificate replacement or unattended production DNS renewal. Test roots
were confined to the QA client; system trust, operational ACME directories and
production listeners/proxies were unchanged.

Each temporary QA session was removed after its checks. Observation cleanup was

Stable still requires a separate explicit owner decision for these limits:

- No supported client capacity, throughput/UDP-loss envelope or disk-sync sizing
  benchmark; the shortened, light-load observation does not close that gate.
- No independent full-stack restore after source-host loss; matching-key local
  QA database restore is narrower evidence.
- No actual replacement of a production public certificate; the staging renewal
  and isolated QA reload are narrower evidence.
- No network-reachability or censorship-resistance guarantee. Shared-host QA
  does not establish all-client or all-network compatibility.

The final stable candidate/tag must pass its own complete CI, notices/security
checks, publication guards and public-asset verification. Publication and any
later live-server rollout remain separate decisions.
